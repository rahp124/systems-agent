## Design Specificity Verdict

**Content-specific, visually category-interchangeable.** The copy is unusually disciplined: competing contamination, leak, and sensor-fault hypotheses; expected-information selection; synthetic evidence boundaries; and read-only governance are unmistakably specific to this work. The cream and dark-green palette, oversized editorial serif, mono eyebrows, three-card explainer, and dark terminal are familiar climate-tech and AI-research landing-page grammar. The hero contains no network topology, telemetry signal, hypothesis evolution, or investigation path—the mechanism only this agent can own.

The deterministic scan produced six warnings. Two are material: aggressive headline tracking and lime text with insufficient contrast on the light safety section. The side-tab warning is low impact, the two all-caps warnings refer to short eyebrow labels rather than body copy, and the primary-font warning misidentifies Instrument Serif even though DM Sans is the body face.

No reliable visual overlay was produced because the in-app browser could not initialize. HTTP behavior was independently checked, but no desktop/mobile visual inspection is claimed in this run.

## Overall Impression

The page earns trust through unusually honest evidence boundaries and a logical problem → method → evidence → trial → safety sequence. Its biggest opportunity is to make the auditable investigation trail—not an editorial headline or terminal—the first-view thesis and the peak interaction.

## What's Working

- Evidence types are clearly separated by what they can prove, with limitations adjacent to the headline numbers.
- Safety language is concrete and consistently visible across status, lab, safety, and footer copy.
- The three-step method explains a difficult Bayesian process without a wall of theory.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The demo resolves the wrong promise and treats failure as a dead end

**Why it matters:** The opening promises the next observation, but the result leads with final classification and hides candidate action scores. An incorrect seed offers no diagnostic context at the experience's emotional peak.

**Fix:** Make the investigation trail primary: prior → recommended observation and rationale → returned evidence → posterior change → next recommendation. Make classification secondary and turn mismatches into an explanation of residual uncertainty.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P1] Dynamic results are visually legible but semantically inaccessible

**Why it matters:** The result region has no live-region semantics; visual bar widths carry probability; completion and failure are not announced to assistive technology.

**Fix:** Add a status/live region, semantic ordered steps, textual probability changes, accessible progress values, focus placement, and strong focus-visible styles.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit`

### [P2] The visual system does not express the distinctive mechanism

**Why it matters:** Editorial type, cards, and a terminal could represent many research tools. They leave the hypothesis-and-evidence model abstract.

**Fix:** Promote a product-native network and belief trail into the first viewport and reuse its grammar throughout method and results.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`

### [P2] Mobile removes navigation from a long page

**Why it matters:** Below 720px every section link disappears, forcing a full linear traversal.

**Fix:** Preserve compact section navigation, keep the interactive lab directly reachable, and provide a return path after the run.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

### [P2] Audience and terminology are insufficiently staged

**Why it matters:** Utility decision-makers, researchers, and operators need different proof, while unexplained acronyms increase comprehension cost.

**Fix:** Establish a plain-language primary path with contextual definitions and progressively disclosed research detail.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable onboard`

## Persona Red Flags

- **Jordan, first-timer:** The first action is clear, but unexplained terminology and the absence of a plain-language post-run summary create an avoidable comprehension cliff.
- **Riley, stress tester:** Decimal seeds pass browser validation but fail server validation; all server failures are disguised as a seed-range error; incorrect classifications lack stopping rationale or remaining uncertainty.
- **Casey, distracted mobile user:** Navigation disappears, the page becomes a long scroll, run state is lost on refresh, and the terminal creates nested scrolling.
- **Utility evaluator:** The interface foregrounds classification when the advisory product promise is the next observation, rationale, provenance, confidence, and escalation boundary.
- **Water-systems researcher:** The caveats are strong, but method metadata and replication boundaries are buried in raw linked files.

## Minor Observations

- Add a skip link and active navigation state.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Avoid relying on remote fonts for a reliable demo.
- State that seeds must be whole numbers.
- Label raw Markdown and JSON destinations by format.
- Test the fixed result-bar grid and nested scrolling at narrow widths.

## Questions to Consider

- Should the first viewport behave more like an investigation instrument than a landing-page hero?
- Should utility evaluators and researchers share one opening proof sequence, then diverge into tailored depth?
- Can an incorrect run become the most persuasive demonstration by showing why the agent was wrong and which observation could resolve the uncertainty?
